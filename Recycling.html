Recycling for


a Greener Future

Every year, billions of tonnes of waste end up in landfills and oceans, harming ecosystems and wasting valuable resources. By recycling plastics, glass, paper, and electronics, we give materials a second life — but understanding what can and cannot be recycled is the first step toward real change.





First, not everything that carries a recycling symbol can actually be recycled through local collection systems.

Some plastics, such as soft packaging, plastic film, or certain tubs and trays, may not be accepted by your local council. Even though they display the familiar triangle symbol, recycling rules vary by region.

It means that putting these items in the wrong bin can cause problems down the line.





Second, contamination is one of the biggest challenges in recycling. When non-recyclable or dirty items—

greasy pizza boxes or food-covered containers end up in the recycling bin, they can spoil an entire batch.
In some cases, the whole load may be rejected and sent to landfill instead of being properly recycled.

This is why cleaning and sorting items correctly is so important.


Understanding which materials can truly be recycled and ensuring that items are clean and properly sorted are both essential to making the process work effectively. By being more careful and informed about what we put into our recycling bins, we can help reduce waste, prevent contamination, and support a more sustainable future for everyone.
